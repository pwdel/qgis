## Exercise sequence

1. Choose circuit A or B and read its lake sequence. Identify your starting area, the 1 km grid, and the printed scale.
2. Mark a water leg with pencil. Measure its grid bearing and distance. Convert the bearing using the dated correction on the sheet.
3. Choose two mapped high points or distinct headlands, draw back-bearings and compare their intersection with the intended observation point. The instructor checks actual terrain/landmark visibility before any field use.
4. Use the broad lettered shore regions to explain where you would search for a carry. Compare that reasoning with the hidden recorded and approximate passage layers.
5. Turn on one instructor circuit at a time. Compare the student construction to its hypothetical water legs, paying attention to islands and bays.

The optional astrolabe idea is not a prerequisite for these charts. A separate instrument-specific lesson would need the actual instrument and intended observation method; no celestial fix is claimed by this map.

## Geometry qualifications

Recorded trail endpoints may sit slightly inside or outside a shoreline polygon. Water-leg endpoints are derived shoreline candidates. There is no fabricated connecting segment presented as surveyed geometry. The instructor layers retain true source endpoints, approximate GPX passages and candidate landings separately. The logical lake circuits close at Clearwater, while incomplete source carry geometry remains explicitly incomplete.
